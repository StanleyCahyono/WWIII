# Claude Code master prompt | World Command: World 120

Build a private, single-player, world-scale grand strategy game for me. I want to play as one country in a fictional modern global conflict. Every other country must be a computer player with its own economy, limited information, diplomacy and objectives. Use the official Conflict of Nations wiki as the functional reference for the depth of the campaign and its interface. Write original code, use original presentation, and ship a game I can actually play from choosing a country through the last day of the campaign.

Work in my local project folder. Leave GitHub setup, pushes, remote branches, account connections and hosting for a later request. Run the game on my machine. Other human players, public rooms, shared servers, subscription checks, payment flows, telemetry and external AI services are outside this product. The only human seat belongs to me. Bots must continue playing even if I spend the entire campaign observing them. A bot country remains an independent actor when it becomes my coalition partner.

Treat this entire document, including the reference sections at the end, as one implementation prompt. The source notes describe the commercial game's historical interface. The instructions below specify what I want you to build. A source feature involving another person becomes a local interaction with a computer country where appropriate. An account or storefront feature with no use in a private campaign gets a documented disposition. The user-facing name **World Command** is a working title.

## The World 120 campaign

Ship one selectable campaign named **World 120**. The player picks any one of the following 120 countries. Instantiate 119 independent country controllers and one human controller. Geography, country strengths and the opening calendar are fixed. A seed changes diplomatic personalities, market activity and event draws within documented bounds. A new save starts another playthrough of this same campaign. The screen after New Game is **Choose your country**. Compact fixtures belong exclusively to automated tests. The difficulty selector sets planning effort for all 119 computer countries. It does not change the roster or award resources. Tutorial tasks appear in the player's actual World 120 save.

## Asymmetric countries and the U.S. technology package

Build 120 authored starting profiles. **The U.S. starts first overall.** Normalize the strongest non-U.S. country's composite opening power to 100. Set the U.S. to **118**, with an acceptance band of **115–121** after data rounding. Use actual start assets. Do not add an invisible “USA strength” multiplier or count the same trait twice. Compute the metric before diplomatic treaties or random events. Persist the calculation and show a readable breakdown in the national dossier. This is a target for the opening position, not a bonus that keeps applying after the game starts.

| Dimension | Weight | Compute from the actual opening save |
| --- | ---: | --- |

| Ready formations | 25% | Sum the replacement value of fielded formations weighted by readiness. Use standard catalog prices and exclude cargo twice-counted inside a formation. |
| Research capacity | 20% | Cost-weighted share of useful unlocked nodes plus funded research throughput across a fixed planning horizon. |

For each dimension score the U.S. and all rivals against the same reference basket. Handle a zero denominator as a configuration error. Publish the five raw values, normalization constants and nearest rival. The requirement concerns the composite: peers can lead individual dimensions. A $1 change in the reference basket must not cause an unrelated country's starting forces to change.

## Missile and hypersonic gameplay

Give the player a credible catalog, finite stocks, visible industrial commitments and consistent reports. Use the real distinctions among the families in the encyclopedia. Implement launches, travel intervals, defense checks and damage as **fictional, turn-scale game events** using sector IDs, abstract ratings and original balance values. All names, costs and timings for playable equipment are authored for this game. The source material supplies terminology. It does not calibrate effects against actual cities, platforms or defenses.

| Catalog family | Encyclopedia meaning | Distinct role in the game's economy and UI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional cruise missile | A powered cruise-missile family; “cruise” identifies the family, while “conventional” labels the payload in this catalog. | A mature research path with relatively affordable production, a middle-duration game timer and a readable inventory/replenishment cycle. |
| Ballistic missile | A ballistic family. Some ballistic systems reach hypersonic speeds; the speed descriptor alone does not classify an item as a glide vehicle. | Show a separately researched abstract strategic token, a larger production commitment and a distinct event icon after classification. |
| Hypersonic glide vehicle (HGV) | A booster carries the vehicle before its glide phase. Use “boost-glide system” when describing the complete fictional inventory item. | Two-step program unlock and small batch sizes. Display a schematic phase label only where visible to the viewer. Expensive shots create a budget and stock-management decision. |
| Hypersonic cruise missile (HCM) | A powered, air-breathing hypersonic cruise family. Hypersonic generally refers to Mach 5 or above. | A separate late-game research branch with a different production-input mix and a short campaign timer. A common materials prerequisite does not grant the other family. |

Keep speed class, flight family, payload, program maturity, availability, cost and game accuracy as separate attributes. Use “prototype,” “qualified,” “in production,” “ready” and “expended” in their proper sequence. A groundbreaking research result creates a blueprint. Only a completed production job creates inventory. Fictional prototype art must look different from a ready token. Attach campaign-era and provenance fields to each catalog record. Never treat a publicly announced development program as a verified deployed stock in the opening campaign.

**Resolution and reporting.** Store a launch as a uniquely identified simulation event. Validate fictional sector eligibility and inventory on commit. One successful action debits one ready item. Resolve each item exactly once as arrived with effect, arrived with limited effect, intercepted, or failed. A faction lacking an appropriate observation receives only its permitted report, possibly “effect unconfirmed.” Separate the underlying outcome from what either side knows about it. The game clock can pause or accelerate without altering the result. A visible map animation is an abstract origin-to-sector event illustration and carries no steering controls.

**What “accurate” means to the player.** An accuracy label is conditional on an item having arrived and on the specified game context; the headline chance of a useful effect includes earlier game failure states. Define both in the tooltip. Use fictional bands for game balance. Draw random events from a saved seed with stable per-event streams. A high speed category confers no automatic improvement in accuracy, damage or certainty of the report. List the possible results before commitment, display inventory immediately afterward and keep denied observations private. In an isolated statistical test with 10,000 synthetic launches and an advertised game probability of 0.70, verify the observed proportion lies within three binomial standard errors of the input. Repeat for low and high probabilities. A deterministic fixture tests branches exactly, including a failure after an accepted order; the statistical check catches mislabeled probabilities, not physics errors. The sum of exclusive terminal probabilities must equal one.

**Why build one?** Each missile family needs a meaningful opportunity cost. Reserve a production slot, spend scarce inputs and pay a visible replacement time. Publish inventory ceilings, batch cost and current build backlog. A late-game U.S. research start makes entry possible earlier; production competes with its air and naval programs. Two peer countries can also pursue hypersonic programs through reachable research. Smaller countries can win through industry, trade, coalition membership and territorial objectives without producing a single missile. The AI values a prospective order in campaign points and resource cost relative to alternative actions. Include one seeded example where a bot cancels a proposed procurement because its trade position deteriorates.

## Depth, pacing and acceptance for this edition

- **Economy.** Forecast the next 24 and 72 game hours. Distinguish stock on hand, spendable stock, reserved goods and net flows. Market orders have finite counterparties and partial fills. An industrial disruption affects its producing city and flows through the ledger. A peace agreement affects access at its published effective time. The first economic report should explain one choice specific to the selected nation.
- **Readiness and maintenance.** Track formation readiness, supply condition, replacements and time in maintenance. An expensive fleet can occupy the top slot on paper yet be unavailable this week. The force screen shows fielded, ready and committed strength separately. The U.S. opening lead makes this distinction visible in its first turn.
- **AI at 120-country scale.** Use one scheduler with staggered economic, diplomatic and operational reviews and a reserved per-country planning budget. Event triggers wake affected countries. Test 119 bots for starvation, including distant states outside the human viewport. Local conflicts and trade continue elsewhere while I inspect a city. A small state can seek access, a coalition, or a trade agreement before considering a war. Count useful decisions by type and state; an AI doing repeated no-ops fails the activity check.
- **Information and diplomacy.** Record a private trust history per pair, including fulfilled and broken agreements, with visible reasons for material shifts. Distinguish trade, military passage, shared reports and coalition membership in data and display. A right-of-way agreement changes accessibility; it does not reclassify every foreign formation as friendly. Changing a relationship refreshes only the viewer's legitimate frames and identification fields.
- **Readability and pace.** Default to a new-game briefing with three specific decisions, each linked to the relevant screen. Bundle routine completions into a daily digest. Reserve interrupt-and-pause defaults for a direct declaration of war on the human, a capital status change and game end. Let the player change interruption preferences. Group follow-up events without losing the underlying audit trail.
- **Performance.** Benchmark the release world at 1440 × 900 with roughly 2,000 active stacks and all 120 countries. Target a p95 map-input response under 100 ms, steady pan at 45 fps or better on a declared reference laptop, and a visible autosave result within two seconds under normal load. Record cold load, median and p95 simulation-day duration, save size, peak memory, OS, browser and hardware. Run separate tests on a 390 × 844 layout, with its own measured results. Accelerated campaigns must leave the UI responsive; move heavy pathfinding and simulation work into a worker if profiling warrants it.

## Acceptance walkthrough for the finished interface

1. Start a local campaign, choose a nation, then reload. Verify one human controller and the same computer countries, game time, ownership and resource balances.
2. Select a province, cycle cities, inspect its morale factors, then use the administration drawer to select two eligible cities and one occupied city. Review and submit a bulk mobilization. Compare total spent and displayed queues to the preview.
3. Select an army, inspect one unit's terrain table, preview a route across a neutral border, and cancel. Confirm both countries retain their original relationship and the previous order survives.
4. Submit a new route, step the simulation and inspect ETA, event history, the current destination and capture eligibility. Confirm a neutral observer sees only events or units it can legitimately observe.
5. Open resources, inspect upkeep, enter the market and post a sell order. Simulate a partial AI fill and cancel the rest. Confirm stock, cash, reserved balance, open orders and history reconcile before and after reload.
6. Generate a report, move the observed computer stack and advance one day. Confirm the report timestamp, frozen location, expiration and historical entry. Search and filtered map layers must not reveal its current position.
7. Send an AI country an offer, save and resume, then process the response. The two diplomatic views, coalition access and related messages must agree. Verify duplicate acceptance applies its effect once.
8. Use only the keyboard to open a city, inspect an order, cancel that order and return to the map. Repeat with a touch viewport. Every disabled action gives a reason and all dialogs can be dismissed.
9. Reach the scenario target and inspect the results. Restore an archived save and resume it without altering that completed campaign. Confirm the game works with external network access disabled.

## Economy, territory and government

**Wiki behavior.** The seven ordinary resource accounts are supplies, components, manpower, rare materials, fuel, electronics and money. Research, construction, mobilization and standing forces draw on different accounts. Production depends on population, morale, buildings and territorial status. The published table gives output factors of 100% homeland, 25% occupied and 50% annexed for qualifying cities. A captured city permits construction. Annexation opens standard mobilization and increases output. It is lost on subsequent capture. Population also contributes to VP. Low morale affects output and completion times. Several influences can move the morale target away from current morale, including shortages, distance from headquarters, neighboring conditions, hostilities and population losses. [Production](https://wiki.conflictnations.com/Production) · [Provinces](https://wiki.conflictnations.com/Provinces)

**Solo design.** Track gross production, upkeep, queued work, market escrow, spendable stock and net rate independently in fixed-point units. Display each cost as a resource vector and highlight exactly which inputs are short. Record production over elapsed simulation time. Apply the territory factor once. In a reference test, if adjusted output before status is 800/day, homeland yields 800, occupation 200 and annexation 400. Display current and target morale together, list signed factors, and preview the local effect of changing the capital or constructing a hospital. Explain stocks collapsing from expenditure, upkeep, reserved offers and simulated corruption in the ledger.

**Territory actions.** Armies capture a province at its control node when a surviving unit has capture capability. Cities use the same possession model and can contain multiple buildings. Geography has both ownership and access permissions. Keep original homeland, current owner, annexation, capital status, population, current/target morale, deposits and infrastructure separately. Empty provinces may still matter for access or scoring. Show a zero-output warning on industry proposals for provinces with no suitable deposit. Provinces in the wiki support outposts, airfields, field hospitals, local industry, military logistics and coastal pontoons. Surface role, construction progress, operating health and damage on inspection. Keep the ten documented terrain types in the registry: open ground, mountains, forest, urban, suburban, jungle, tundra, desert, high seas and coastal waters. [Province facilities and terrain](https://wiki.conflictnations.com/Provinces)

**Market and capture flow.** Rank market bids/asks by price. Display quantities at each price and the owner's individual orders/history. A sell order reserves the goods immediately. Cancel releases its unfilled remainder. Design example: two AI bidders offer 7 and 5. A human accepting bids with a floor of 4 fills the higher price first until its quantity is exhausted. Record partial fills. On city transfer, mutate owner, occupation status, allowed production and score source atomically. Resolve existing job ownership under an explicit project rule. A garrison may have to fight a revolt while the city stabilizes. The wiki puts ordinary revolt risk around 33% morale or below. Author separate probabilities for the solo ruleset. ## Cross-system checks for integration
